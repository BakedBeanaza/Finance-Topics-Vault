## RRP Rate
- this is a rate imposed by BSP to banks
- the rate at which the BSP borrows money from commercial banks using government securities as collateral. In a "reverse repurchase" agreement, the BSP sells these securities to banks with a commitment to buy them back at a specfici date and rate.
- Technically, it is the "anchor" of the **Interest Rate Corridor (IRC)** system:
	- **Overnight Lending Rate (5.25%):** The "ceiling" or the rate at which banks can borrow from the BSP.
		- it acts as a "ceiling" of sorts for the banks. This is the interest rate that the BSP charges when a commercial bank needs to borrow money from the central bank overnight.
	- **Target RRP Rate (4.75%):** The main policy signal.
	- **Overnight Deposit Rate (4.25%):** The "floor" or the rate banks receive for depositing excess funds with the BSP.
		- it acts as a "floor" of sorts for the market. A bank will never lend money to a risky borrower for less than 4.25% because they can get that exact same rate by simply leaving the money safely with the BSP.
### Purpose
- it is mainly used to;
	- **control inflation**
		- if the inflation is too high from the BSP's target infation rate, BSP raises the RRP which makes it expensive for banks to borrow. Since it is more expensive for banks to borrow, banks might increase their interest rates for this. 
		- now example, if you had a mortgage loan that initially had 7% interest rate, that mortgage's interest rate might increase to 7.5% which makes it more expensive.
		- since you require more money to pay for that loan, you will cut on expenses a bit
		- now imagine many others doing the same thing that you just did, this act makes it that spending activity within the economy lowers and the economy does not overheat
	- **manage liquidity**
		- By adjusting the RRP rate, BSP controls the amount of money circulating in the economy
		- a higher rate encourages banks to keep their money with the BSP instead of loaning it out for lending
	- **signaling**
		- it also acts as a signal about BSP's stance on the economy. A hike signals a "hawkish" or tightening stance, while a cut signals a "dovish" or expansionary stance
			- **hawks** because they are predators--they are aggressive about fighting inflation and they are willing to strike the economy with higher interest rates to keep prices from rising
			- **doves** becase they are symbols of peace, gentleness and harmony--it means that a central banker is prioritizing economic growth and employment; they usually prefer low interest rates (easy money) to keep the "peace" in the labor market. They are more likely to tolerate a little bit of inflation if it means that businesses keep hiring and people keep spending.
